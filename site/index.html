How it works

From one sentence to a rota everyone can read.

An Organiser sets the rota up once. After that, each round is a few clicks: ask, solve, check, publish.

  1. 1

    Say when Shifts happen

    Type the pattern in plain English. A language model translates it, a parser checks the result, and a calendar shows exactly which dates it makes before anything is saved.

  2. 2

    Volunteers say when they're free

    Each volunteer gets their own link. No account, no password: a yes or a no for each date, on their phone, and that's it.

  3. 3

    A solver drafts the rota

    A constraint solver fills every Seat from who is free, holding to the Organisation's rules: how often someone works, the gap between Shifts, who must be on each one.

  4. 4

    People decide; everyone sees it

    The Organiser swaps anyone in or out by hand. Then the rota is published, and each volunteer can subscribe to their own Shifts in their calendar app.

Features

Built for the people who run the rota.

Rules from a library

Cap how often anyone works, keep a gap between Shifts, insist every Shift has someone of a given gender. Pick a rule, give it a number.

The rules bind the solver, not you

An Organiser can put anyone on any Shift. The rules shape the draft; people make the call.

Roles and Seats

Say how many of each Role a Shift needs. The solver only seats people in Roles they hold.

Exceptions in plain English

“Start at 11 on Christmas Eve”, “two extra on the first Saturday in December”: exceptions to the pattern are written the same way as the pattern.

See everyone's answers at once

The responses grid lays every volunteer against every Shift, so gaps show up before the solver runs.

One server, many Organisations

Each Organisation lives at its own address, with its own data and its own Organisers.

Under the hood

A language model you can trust with a calendar.

The translator is held to the same standard as the rest of the code. A parser checks every reply before it's shown, and an eval suite grades every model by the Shifts it actually produces.

⟲

Parser-checked retries

A reply the parser refuses goes back to the model with the parser's own message. Three tries, then a clear error rather than a guess.

3 tries
and 30 seconds for all of them
◎

Graded by Shifts, not text

Evals compare the dates a translation produces over a year, so two ways of writing the same pattern both pass. Prompt injection and repairs are cases too.

43 cases
each run 5 times per model
⚖

A matrix across providers

Every candidate model, from Anthropic and Mistral, is compared on accuracy, cost and speed, with a large model judging the explanations.

2 providers
one interface, picked by tier

The reasoning is written down: plain-English patterns, models by provider and tier, the rules bind the solver, and every other decision.
